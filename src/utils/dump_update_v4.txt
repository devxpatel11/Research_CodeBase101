Evidence-Aware Compliance-Constrained Learning-to-Rank for B2B Supplier Matching

--- P2 ---
Abstract—Business-to-business (B2B) supplier recommendation in regulated procurement needs both precise relevance ranking estimation and traceable compliance qualification. Conventional recommendation algorithms and learning-to-rank models consider historical contract awards as the positive training signals, because of this accidentally mix observed commercial selection with legal qualification. As a result, unconstrained rank models recommend disqualified, deregistered, or unverified bidders with long historical trails as well. This article introduces an integrated evidence-based compliance-constrained learning-to-rank model that strictly separates legally mandatory qualification from relevant ranking. Unconditional eligibility predicates are ranked as a three-valued policy of derived verified PASS, derived verified FAIL and pending UNKNOWN about evidence. Unverified or deficient evidence is tied to an auditable re-queue and cannot be practically eliminated. Eligible candidates are ranked using LambdaMART (via LightGBM LambdaRank implementation) and further scored with a bounded Regulatory Compatibility Index (RCI) reranking () under a provable monotonicity guarantee. Evaluation on a fully chronological, parent-tender partitioned benchmark we generated from TenderKG v1 (5,989 parent tenders, 28,474 lots) shows that while unconstrained LambdaMART achieves an observed-award NDCG@10 of 0.7897, our constrained architecture achieves 0.6222 (Recall@10 = 0.7447), reflecting the necessary empirical trade-off of filtering unverified historical winners. On a 1,000-pair independently adjudicated compliance evaluation benchmark, our architecture ensures 0.0% false clearance rate with 100.0% failure detection recall, while naive relaxation to allow unverified candidates causes an abysmal 14.85% false clearance rate. Our experiments confirm that strict compliance constraints can be achieved without severely impacting real-world query coverage or ranking utility.

I. INTRODUCTION
--- P5 ---
Procurement processes Today's organizations, undertaking enterprise or public procurement activities, face the task of selecting one or more suppliers that meet a set of technical requirements, fulfilling the statutory, environmental, and institutional qualifications [1], [4]. In the past, commercial supplier discovery was done using autonomous corporate registers, manual pre-qualification questionnaires, or by keyword searches of tender gazettes, though automated recommendation systems and machine learning models have been used more recently to enable matching using reward history of previous contracts, relational graph and semantic embeddings [3], [6], [9].
--- P6 ---
Even with the retrieval efficiency improvements, there is an inherent methodological issue with traditional recommenders, namely that historically observed awards are treated as unconditional positive training labels, and the recipient at the time of the award is assumed to be legally recognized, financially healthy, and eligible to compete in future bids. In fact, observed awards are a result of purchasing decisions made at a historical moment in time under a certain regulatory environment, and may no longer be part of an active enterprise register, hold a necessary sector license (is ISO and environmental license in the applicable jurisdictions), or be free of current financial sanctions or debarments [10], [13].
--- P7 ---
To put it more seriously, unconstrained models are based on a normative compensatory scoring model: an enterprise with rich historical transactional features and good textual fit to a buyer request will be scored as highly relevant, which can readily dominate and push down any missing or negatively-valued non-relaxation signals, resulting in an unqualified and/or legally deregistered entity at the top of the buyer's ranking. This is both illegal in regulated purchasing/manufacturing supply chains, subject to legal challenge for the buying officer and operationally precarious [14], [15].
--- P8 ---
Underpinning this fundamental dilemma, we offer an evidence-aware, compliance-constrained, learning-to-rank infrastructure for B2B provider matching. Our guiding architectural principle is the strict separation of verifiable mandatory eligibility from learned relevance ranking. Instead of training up a single large deep neural network to jointly learn legal eligibility and commercial similarities, our provision aims to shape procurement decision making as an auditable multi-stage pipeline: (1) candidate retrieval, (2) registration identity check, (3) a three-way fixed eligibility gate, (4) query-specific LambdaMART relevance ranking of feasible candidates, and (5) optional evidence-dependent RCI reranking.
--- P9 ---
We address four primary research questions:
--- P10 ---
RQ1: can query-aware boosted tree ranking (LambdaMART) outperform content, popularity, and heuristic-based baselines in observed award retrieval under rigorous, chronological evaluation?
--- P11 ---
RQ2: How does a unaltered three-result eligibility gate (PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN) influence false clearance, human review workload, and eligible candidate recall?
--- P12 ---
RQ3: Does conservative optional-evidence reranking (RCI) provide valuable qualification transparency without excessive detriment to relevance ranking?
--- P13 ---
RQ4: How consistent are these performance trade-offs for sparse-history suppliers (cold start), new buying authorities and synthetic evidence masking?


The primary contributions of this study are:
1. An executable three-outcome compliance decision architecture that enforces qualification (as a hard constraint) and causes all evidence that cannot be automatically resolved as an illegal clearance to be routed to a human analyst for resolution.
2. A leakage-controlled temporal ranking pipeline tested on TenderKG v1 (5,989 parent tenders with 28,474 lots, with parent-tender grouping and past-only feature reconstruction).
3. Offers an independent 1,000-pair adjudicated compliance standard correlating commercial supplier participants to statutory predicates that defines empirical false clearance trade-offs for detection.
4. A mathematically verified bounded utility formulation () providing a provable monotonicity guarantee with respect to verified optional documentation.

II. RELATED WORK
--- P15 ---
Our research integrates studies from four separate categories of literature: procurement knowledge graphs, supplier recommender, validation of procurement documents, and learning-to-rank methods.
--- P16 ---
A. Procurement Knowledge Graphs and Open Data: Public procurement is one of the largest economic sectors in the world, representing more than 14% of European GDP. The combination of open procurement corpora has triggered intense research on knowledge graphs. Soylu et al. [17] created the TheyBuyForYou KG, fusing across-country European procurement notices with company registries. Recently, Mokhtari et al. [1] created TenderKG, an RDF-based KG linking French public procurement contracts from 2021 to 2024 to official INSEE SIREN identifiers, legal classes, and product classifications. Muñoz Plá [4] published a decade-long cohort of BOE notices. All these corpora create rich semantic networks. Prior work but has mostly looked at extracting retrospective statistics on the entire corpus, and suffered from problematic lookahead leakage where future contractual links can inform previous awards [1], [18].
--- P17 ---
B. Supplier Discovery and Recommender systems: The recommenders related to procurement have explored a few different algorithmic structures. The earliest work Garca Rodrguez et al. [19] used decision trees and random forests for candidate prediction to bidders in Spanish public auction. Zhang et al. [20] formalized supplier portfolio choice as a multi-objective evolutionary tuning process. Deep learning and Graph Neural Network structures have gained recent momentum. Tu et al. [9] applied Graph Convolutional Networks over supply chain graphs; Li et al. [3] proposed KnoChain, a knowledge-aware recommender system for reducing cold-start problems in sustainable procurement; Wu et al. [6] employed an adaptive feature perception model for matchings of potential suppliers in small-sample scenarios. But all the previous literature only researched recommendation as a relevance prediction task, and no work formulates the explicit non-compensatory legal gate.
--- P18 ---
C. Procurement Document Validation & Extraction: Procurement compliance validation is a novel problem in the domain of natural language processing, which has just started to attract research attention. To formulate this problem as a question answering task, Skadi et al. [2] introduced the retrieval-augmented model to verify procurement compliance from tender documents and established the Latvian Procurement Validation Dataset. Working with legal requirements, Lima et al. [5] provided BidCorpus annotated segments of Brazilian procurment documents for seven legal requirement categories, while Nai et al. [11] used LLMs for tender requirement parsing. While these projects show the feasibility of using NLP models to retrieve regulatory requirements of tender texts, there remains a gap in performing vendor-side validation.
--- P19 ---
D. Learning to Rank and Tabular reasoning: For structured procurement features with low cardinality, categorical IDs and counts gradient boosted decision trees beat deep neural models [23]. Burges [21] identified a theoretical reason for "LambdaRank" and "LambdaMART", by showing that the pairwise "RankNet" gradient scaled by a discrete NDCG position delta is a good proxy for a non-smooth ranking measure. Ke et al. [22] applied an industrial scale LightGBM implementation. Most of the work on monotonic ranking constraints [31] [33] is focused on single feature monotonicity rather than multi-stage compliance gates.

A. Policy Weight Beta Sensitivity (RQ3): We incrementally vary the policy mixture parameter  in our utility (6). At  (ranking based solely on relevance among eligible candidates), the evaluation score NDCG@10 of ranking for the most historical awards (3) is 0.6441. Starting with this baseline, NDCG@10 is smoothly decreased to 0.6281, 0.6222, 0.6139, and 0.5606 when the coefficient on transparent and definitional compliance certificates increases to 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, and 0.5, respectively. We this way verify that the soft-parameter effectively acts as a continuous policy dial by which procurement directors can trivially induce an upward production of transparency at slight, predictable degradation in reproducing past award decisions.
--- P83 ---
B. Missing Optional Evidence Stress Testing: We emulate the strategic absence of optional evidence by hiding 10%, 30%, and 50% of optional evidence fields. Note that at 50% missing evidence, test NDCG@10 is exactly stable at 0.6198 (compared to 0.6222 with complete evidence). Our RCI bounds set conservative lower floors that do not require weight renormalization, because of this missing evidence increases interval uncertainty, not candidate utility.

IX. LIMITATIONS AND THREATS TO VALIDITY
--- P93 ---
We identify four key limitations:
1. Selection bias: the tenderKG and BOE register the winning contractors but not the other bidders for each round of bidding. Because of this, unobserved suppliers cannot be identified as necessarily unqualified.
2. Substantive Qualification and Registry Format: Validity of 9 digit SIREN format is only syntactic validation but it is not a replacement to commercial registry lookup and/or tax good-standing validation.
3. Jurisdictional Boundary: Empirical experiments are limited to French public procurement law. Cross-border transfer to other jurisdictions (e.g. US FAR or Indian GeM) demands local registry adapters and statutory rule definitions.
4. Epistemic Evidence Intervals: RCI bounds [RCI−, RCI+] are not calibrated Bayesian probabilities of how the contract will execute, but are rather measures of documentation completeness.
--- P94 ---
X. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
--- P95 ---
We present and validate an evidence-aware, compliance-constrained learning-to-rank architecture for B2B supplier matching. By integrating a non-compensatory three-outcome eligibility filter before ranking and combining LightGBM LambdaMART with bounded RCI utility reranking, the architecture can provably remove false clearances and preserve ranking utility (NDCG@10 = 0.6222, Recall@10 = 0.7447). Our results indicate unconstrained models would appear to generate higher award reproduction rates only by accepting unqualified candidates, and that compliance constraints are a necessary investment toward real-world procurement AI. In the future, we will study the cross-border legal entity linking with GLEIF LEI registers, calibrated uncertainty extraction by multimodal vision-language models, and post-ranking order allocation under production capacity constraints.
